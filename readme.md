# [Work Mode Identification] Multi-time Series Feature Extraction and Clustering Framework for Multifunctional Radar Work Mode Identification with Datasets Included

Multifunctional Radar (MFRs) are complex sensors with strong flexibility and adaptability in adjusting their emission waveforms and control parameters. Recognizing the operational modes of MFRs based on captured pulse sequences plays a crucial role in interpreting the functional purpose and threat intentions of non-cooperative MFRs. However, as the flexibility of MFRs continues to increase, new modulation methods and control parameters lead to the emergence of radar operational modes that can degrade or even render supervised classification methods ineffective. Consequently, unsupervised learning and clustering of MFR pulse sequences have become urgent and essential. This paper establishes a unified framework for multivariate time series feature extraction and clustering for the recognition of operational modes of MFRs. Firstly, a feature set is collected by combining multiple features, which includes features extracted through recurrent autoencoders via deep learning, multidimensional time series tools, and manually designed features for modulation between radar pulses. Subsequently, various feature selection algorithms and different clustering and classification methods are integrated to select the "optimal" feature subset. Finally, the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed framework and selected features are validated through both simulated datasets and real-world datasets. In a simulated dataset containing 20 operational modes, under the most severe non-ideal conditions, the purity of clustering reached 73.46%, and the normalized mutual information (NMI) was 84.28%; in a real-world dataset containing 7 operational modes, the purity of clustering reached 86.96%, and the NMI was 90.10%.
Introduction With the development of electronic scanning arrays such as phased arrays, modern multifunctional radars (MFRs) are capable of performing multiple tasks simultaneously along the time axis. Additionally, MFRs can adaptively select or optimize between pulse-to-pulse and intra-pulse modulation and control parameters based on their perception of the operational environment to meet higher-level task requirements [1-4]. The modulation patterns for each specific task feature vary significantly in terms of control parameters such as pulse repetition interval (PRI), pulse width (PW), and radio frequency (RF) [5-7]. Recognizing and analyzing the operational modes of MFRs is challenging, posing an urgent challenge to modern electronic receivers.
The primary and fundamental step to effectively identify the operational modes and intent of multifunctional radars is to model the system effectively. Generally, multifunctional radars are complex systems whose signal generation mechanisms can be hierarchically modeled [6-11]. Syntactic models were early attempts to describe the behavior of multifunctional radars [6,8,10]. However, firstly, recognition based on hierarchical models requires prior information about all basic elements and their transformation rules in each layer, which is difficult to fully obtain in non-cooperative applications. Secondly, these recognition methods encode all acquired prior knowledge into nearly fixed matching templates, which then fit captured signals based on prior information, which is challenging to keep up with the flexibility of newly developed multifunctional radars (such as radio definition radar or cognitive radar) in terms of programmable parameters [12-15]. Subsequently, some control parameter (i.e. PRI, RF, and PW) inter-pulse modulation patterns are considered as more intrinsic features distinguishing different operational modes, which provides a promising direction for exploring the mode intent of multifunctional radars [16]. With the latest advances in machine learning and deep learning, many pulse-inter-pulse classification methods have been designed to handle the identification problem of modern multifunctional radars [16-28]. In the author's previous research [29], a sequence-to-sequence classifier was proposed to address the pulse-level identification problem of operational modes, where operational modes are defined as different modulation combinations of multiple control parameters. Fine-grained recognition results can further reveal the mode transitions of multifunctional radars. From these studies, it can be concluded that deep neural networks can efficiently and accurately solve the supervised classification problem of pulse-inter-pulse modulation.
However, the use of pre-acquired training data for supervised learning limits its potential in practical applications of multifunctional radar signals. Firstly, acquiring sufficient training data for numerous programmable operating modes in real complex electromagnetic environments is complex and laborious, and may even be impossible for some special modes that only occur in emergency situations. Secondly, considering the development of multifunctional radars themselves, new adaptive sequence patterns will continuously emerge. A deep learning classifier pretrained through supervised learning cannot guarantee its effectiveness in all cases. The increased freedom of multifunctional radars brings more challenges to supervised recognition methods. Finally, but equally important, the cognitive capabilities of multifunctional radars are becoming a reality [12,14,15,30,31]. They can work with finer patterns under the same modulation but different parameters, meeting different performance requirements. Therefore, researching recognition methods with less prior information is of great significance.
Generally, the intercepted radar pulse sequences are represented by Pattern Description Word (PDW) sequences. In the field of unsupervised learning, Guan [32] adopts the concept of Needleman-Wunsch algorithm to utilize sequence alignment for acquiring search mode rules of multifunctional radars; Fang [33] proposed an unsupervised change point detection algorithm based on Bayesian criterion to identify the working modes of multifunctional radars; Liu [34] introduced a semantic encoding model and an encoding strategy optimization method for multifunctional radar pulse sequences, which can automatically discover the sequence patterns of the pulse sequences signals of multifunctional radars and represent them in the simplest form for extraction. However, these methods are limited in their application scope for the working modes of multifunctional radars and require further research to adapt to new radar systems with agile modulation and other modulation methods. Treating PDW sequences as multivariate time series (MTS), we can study unsupervised feature extraction and clustering methods to obtain more general and less prior information solutions. Recently, some studies have considered the time series clustering of radar signal [22,35]. Guillaume [35] focused on clustering pulse sequences from different radar radiation sources and used means to represent the time series features of the pulse sequences. Since the parameter values of different radiation sources have distinguishability in the high-dimensional PDW space, their method achieved satisfactory performance. However, for multifunctional radars, the parameter values of different working modes are close or overlapping, which would decrease the performance of these methods. In [22], a parameter model for different PRI modulation was established and three methods for subsequence clustering of working mode subsequences of multifunctional radars were proposed. But clustering of multivariate time series still needs further research. In fact, there has been research on clustering of multivariate time series in many other fields. There are many comprehensive reviews on clustering of time series [36-40] and various studies on various methods of extracting or clustering features of multivariate time series [41-49]. However, according to our knowledge, research on identifying multifunctional radar pulse sequences from the perspective of clustering multivariate time series is still lacking.
This paper focuses on establishing a multi-modal time series feature extraction and clustering framework for the purpose of extracting optimal features for multifunctional radar operating modes. The clustering framework includes five steps: preprocessing, feature extraction, feature selection, recognition, and evaluation. In the feature extraction phase, both manual feature engineering and deep learning feature extraction methods were studied, including: (1) handcrafted feature engineering for PRI modulation type identification; (2) handcrafted feature engineering for multi-modal time series from extensive design; (3) unsupervised automatic feature engineering based on deep neural networks. Extensive experiments have validated the effectiveness of the proposed clustering framework, with experimental results confirming the superiority of the proposed framework and selected features. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:  
A multi-modal time series feature extraction and clustering framework for multifunctional radar pulse sequences has been designed.  
The proposed framework and its various implementations have been evaluated and compared, using effective and advanced methods in each implementation.  
The experimental dataset contains multiple radar modulation modes, making the selected features more universally applicable.  
The remainder of this paper is arranged as follows: Section 2 describes the formulation of the multi-modal time series feature extraction and clustering task for multifunctional radar; Section 3 introduces the proposed framework and corresponding implementation details; Section 4 provides a description of the data, experimental design, and experimental results; finally, Section 5 concludes the paper and provides guidance for future work.

This paper establishes a unified clustering framework for multifunctional radar time series and performs feature selection on a large number of time series features. Based on the existing progress in time series research and machine learning, this paper considers features extracted through deep learning based on autoencoders, multidimensional time series features, and traditionally designed features for pulse-intermodulation types in radar parameters. A feature set is formed by combining these methods. Subsequently, the NSGA-II and SFS feature selection algorithms are combined with various clustering and classification methods to optimize the features. Finally, extensive simulations on pulse sequence datasets validate the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed framework and selected feature set.
Given the increased degrees of freedom in modern multifunctional radars, unsupervised identification of their operational modes is of significant importance in modern electromagnetic environments. There are many future works that can be conducted: First, more adaptive and accurate clustering methods need to be developed to address the irregular distribution of different operational modes in high-dimensional feature spaces; second, based on the findings of this study, subsequence clustering methods should be explored for clustering pulse sequences containing multiple consecutive radar operational modes; finally, probabilistic graph models should be studied to address potential dependencies between different variables in the application of multifunctional radars.
